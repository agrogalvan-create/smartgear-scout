How to choose a water pressure booster pump for your home

Independent buying guide · November 2026. General planning information, not a site-specific plumbing design or a hands-on equipment test.

Before buying: Check whether low pressure is caused by clogged filters, undersized piping, a malfunctioning regulator or inadequate source flow. A booster pump cannot correct every supply problem.

Not sure whether you need a pump? Start with our low water pressure troubleshooting checklist to rule out common restrictions and supply problems.

Measure pressure while water is flowing

Static pressure alone does not describe what happens when multiple fixtures are open. Measure pressure under representative demand and estimate the flow you need in gallons per minute (GPM). The pump must provide the required pressure increase at that flow, not just an impressive maximum pressure rating.

Read the performance curve

Compare the required operating point against the manufacturer's pressure-versus-flow curve. Account for pipe friction, elevation and any treatment equipment. A pump advertised with high maximum pressure may deliver substantially less pressure at your actual demand.

Compare constant-speed and variable-speed controls

Constant-speed systems typically cycle on and off based on pressure control, sometimes using a pressure tank. Variable-speed systems can adjust motor speed to meet changing demand within their operating range. Review minimum flow, pressure control, cycling protection, electrical requirements and service availability for either approach.

Confirm the water source can keep up

Check the municipal supply or storage tank capacity. Directly drawing water from a public water main may be restricted or prohibited by the local utility. Some installations require a break tank, backflow prevention or other safeguards. If the source is a well, evaluate its sustainable yield separately; see our well pump guide.

Review plumbing and installation constraints

  • Maximum pressure ratings of pipes, fittings and fixtures
  • Dry-run and overheating protection
  • Noise, vibration, service access and drainage
  • Electrical supply, grounding and applicable code requirements
  • Backflow prevention and expansion control where required
  • Manufacturer warranty and local parts availability
