Drip irrigation problems: troubleshoot before replacing equipment

Independent technical guide · November 2026. General maintenance guidance, not a site-specific irrigation design or hands-on equipment test.

Some plants are dry while others are wet

First distinguish irrigation distribution from soil conditions. Soil texture, slope, root depth and sun exposure can change the wetting pattern even when emitters discharge similar volumes. Check whether the affected plants share a lateral, a high point or the end of a long run. A clogged emitter or an unsuitable emitter spacing pattern may also explain uneven moisture.

Check filtration and clogging

Small emitter passages can become restricted by suspended solids, biological growth or precipitates. Review the filter type and mesh specification recommended by the emitter manufacturer, inspect filter maintenance indicators and follow the system's approved flushing procedure. Do not assume a stronger pump will fix clogging.

Pressure problems

Drip products are designed for particular operating pressure ranges. Excess pressure may cause leaks or uneven delivery; insufficient pressure can impair distribution. Measure pressure at appropriate accessible points while the zone operates, using suitable equipment. Check pressure regulators and pressure-compensating emitter requirements against published specifications.

Leaks and disconnected tubing

Inspect visible connectors, fittings, punctures and damaged tubing. Unusual puddling may signal a leak, but water movement in soil can obscure the source. Repair with components compatible with the tubing dimensions and pressure rating, and follow manufacturer installation instructions.

Check flow against zone capacity

Add the rated flow of the emitters in a zone, accounting for how many outlets operate simultaneously. Compare with available supply flow and the zone's regulator and filter ratings. A zone that demands more than the supply can deliver may underperform even with clean emitters.

A practical inspection sequence

  1. Record which plants and laterals are affected.
  2. Inspect the water source and filter condition.
  3. Check for visible leaks and damaged fittings.
  4. Compare operating pressure with equipment specifications.
  5. Inspect and flush according to manufacturer instructions.
  6. Reassess wetting patterns after a normal irrigation cycle.
