Rain Sensor Not Working? Irrigation Troubleshooting Checklist

Independent technical guide · November 2026. General educational information, not a hands-on product test or site-specific engineering design.

First identify the sensor type

Traditional rain sensors may use moisture-absorbing discs or other mechanisms. Wireless systems may have a transmitter, receiver and batteries. Some controllers use internet weather data rather than a physical rain sensor. Follow the specific product manual.

When irrigation runs during rain

Check whether sensor bypass is enabled, whether the controller supports the connected sensor, and whether the sensor is exposed to representative rainfall. Obstructions, poor placement or a failed switch may prevent an expected rain delay.

When irrigation stays off after rain

Inspect the controller's sensor status and rain-delay settings. Some sensors require time to dry before irrigation resumes. Persistent shutdown can reflect wiring, receiver power, battery condition or a malfunctioning sensor.

Safe checks

  1. Record the controller and sensor model numbers.
  2. Check documented bypass and sensor-enable settings.
  3. Inspect accessible mounting and visible damage.
  4. Follow the manufacturer's approved test sequence.
  5. Turn off power before wiring inspection and consult a qualified technician if uncertain.

What a sensor cannot fix

A rain sensor does not correct leaking valves, inadequate pressure, mismatched nozzles or uneven distribution. See our zone troubleshooting guide and controller buying checklist.
