How to choose a pressure washer: PSI, GPM and real cleaning needs

Independent buying guide · November 2026. Educational guidance based on general equipment principles; no hands-on testing or sponsored product rankings.

Safety: High-pressure jets can cause serious injection injuries and damage surfaces. Never point the spray at people or animals. Follow the manufacturer's operating instructions and wear appropriate protective equipment.

PSI and GPM measure different things

Pressure, measured in pounds per square inch (PSI), influences how forcefully water strikes a surface. Flow, measured in gallons per minute (GPM), influences how much water carries away loosened dirt. Neither maximum PSI nor motor horsepower alone predicts cleaning results. Compare operating specifications rather than relying only on headline maximum values.

Match equipment to the surface

Painted siding, wood, vehicles, concrete and masonry tolerate different cleaning approaches. Start with the manufacturer's recommended nozzle, pressure and standoff distance, and test an inconspicuous area. Too much pressure can strip paint, damage wood fibers, force water behind cladding or etch softer surfaces.

Electric or gas-powered?

Electric models may suit smaller, intermittent household jobs where power and water are readily available. Gas models can offer different duty cycles and portability but require fuel handling, maintenance and exhaust precautions. Never operate a combustion-engine washer indoors or in a partially enclosed space because of carbon monoxide.

Check water supply and power

Confirm that the supply hose and source can provide the flow required by the washer at its inlet. Inadequate water supply can cause poor performance or pump damage. Check electrical outlet, extension-cord and ground-fault protection requirements for electric equipment; follow the manufacturer rather than improvising adapters.

Understand nozzle choices

Spray patterns vary from concentrated jets to wider fans. Narrower patterns can be more aggressive at the same machine setting. Use only compatible nozzles and accessories. Avoid directing water at electrical fixtures, fragile seals or damaged materials.

Detergents and environmental precautions

Use only cleaning solutions approved for the machine and the surface. Avoid mixing chemicals. Follow local requirements for runoff and wastewater, particularly near storm drains, soil and waterways. Pressure washing can mobilize oil, paint residues and other contaminants.

Before buying

  • Define the surfaces, frequency and scale of the cleaning work.
  • Compare rated operating PSI and GPM, not only advertised maxima.
  • Verify available water flow and electrical or fuel requirements.
  • Check hose length, nozzle compatibility, storage and service access.
  • Review pump maintenance, warranty and replacement parts.
