How to choose a smart irrigation controller for your yard

Independent editorial guide · November 2026. General buying guidance; no hands-on testing or sponsored rankings.

Smart scheduling can help avoid unnecessary watering, but savings depend on climate, system condition, landscape needs, watering rules and correct setup. No controller can fix broken valves, leaks or poor sprinkler coverage by software alone.

Start with your existing irrigation system

Count independently operated stations rather than sprinkler heads. Check whether your current valves use conventional 24 VAC wiring or another control system. Photograph the old controller's wiring before removing anything. Read our valve and wiring compatibility guide before ordering a replacement.

Understand weather-based scheduling

Some controllers adjust watering based on local weather observations or forecasts, while others use on-site sensors or a combination. Forecast-based control depends on network connectivity and the accuracy of local weather data. Check whether the system supports seasonal adjustments, rain delays and restrictions imposed by your water utility.

Check your Wi-Fi and offline options

Measure wireless coverage at the actual installation location, especially inside garages or outdoor enclosures. Verify supported Wi-Fi bands, app requirements and what happens when the internet connection fails. Some controllers retain schedules locally; check each manufacturer's documentation instead of assuming identical behavior.

Choose enough zones without overbuying

Include all active valves, a possible master valve or pump-start relay and realistic future expansion. If your system operates a pump, confirm the controller's relay-output compatibility and have electrical controls assessed by a qualified installer. A low-voltage controller output must not directly energize a pump motor.

Consider sensors and water-use rules

Review compatibility with rain, freeze and soil-moisture sensors. Local utilities may require certain devices or offer rebates for qualifying products, sometimes subject to installation or certification conditions. Verify current program eligibility directly with the utility before purchasing.

Compare total ownership costs

  • Controller and required weatherproof enclosure
  • Optional Wi-Fi modules, sensors or accessories
  • Installation, wiring repair and electrical work
  • Any optional subscriptions or cloud-dependent features
  • Warranty, manufacturer support and replacement availability

Prepare for freezing weather

In regions with freezing winters, smart scheduling is not a substitute for draining or otherwise winterizing the system. Review our sprinkler winterization guide and follow manufacturer instructions for backflow protection and piping.

Before buying: a practical sequence

  1. Document station count, valve type and existing wiring.
  2. Confirm indoor/outdoor mounting and electrical requirements.
  3. Test Wi-Fi signal and check offline behavior.
  4. Review scheduling, sensors and local watering restrictions.
  5. Compare total cost and current manufacturer specifications.
