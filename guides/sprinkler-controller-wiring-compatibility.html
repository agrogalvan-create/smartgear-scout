Will a smart sprinkler controller work with your existing valves?

Independent installation-planning guide · November 2026. This article does not claim hands-on testing and contains no affiliate links.

Safety first: Shut off power before inspecting controller wiring. Do not connect household line voltage to a low-voltage irrigation terminal. Consult a licensed professional where electrical or pump controls are involved.

1. Identify the existing controller and supply

Record the controller model and its labeled input/output ratings. Many residential irrigation controllers operate conventional 24 VAC solenoid valves, but not every system does. Battery-powered latching-solenoid, two-wire decoder and proprietary systems may require different equipment. Confirm the replacement controller explicitly supports your valve type.

2. Count actual stations, not just sprinklers

Each independently controlled valve station usually needs a controller zone output. Count the station wires connected at the existing controller, including spare or planned expansion. A yard with many sprinkler heads may still have only a few zones.

3. Find the common wire

Conventional multi-wire valve systems normally have one common return wire shared among valves and a separate switched conductor per station. Label each conductor before disconnecting the old controller. Wire colors are not universal; do not assume white always means common without tracing and verifying the installation.

4. Check master valves and pump-start relays

A master valve or pump-start relay may use a dedicated MV/P terminal. Verify the new controller supports the required output and the relay's electrical characteristics. A controller output must not be used to directly power a pump motor. Confirm relay wiring, protection and interlocks with the equipment documentation.

5. Review simultaneous valve loads

Compare valve solenoid inrush and holding-current requirements with the controller's per-station and total output ratings. Systems that energize a master valve and a zone valve together must remain within the controller's supported load. Consult manufacturers' technical data rather than relying on a generic compatibility claim.

6. Check location and connectivity

Confirm whether the controller is rated for indoor or outdoor installation and whether an enclosure or separate transformer is required. Test Wi-Fi coverage at the mounting location, supported wireless bands and the behavior of watering schedules during an internet outage.

7. Document before buying

  • Existing controller model and photograph of terminal labels
  • Supply voltage and valve/solenoid type
  • Number of active stations and planned expansion
  • Common wire and any master-valve/pump-start connections
  • Indoor/outdoor installation requirements and Wi-Fi signal
  • Manufacturer wiring diagram, warranty and local installation rules
