Sprinkler runoff: why water pools and how to reduce waste

Independent educational guide · November 2026. This is general irrigation guidance, not a site-specific infiltration test or landscape engineering plan.

Runoff is a mismatch between application and absorption

Sprinklers apply water at a precipitation rate, often expressed in inches per hour. Soil can absorb water only at a limited rate, which changes with moisture, compaction, surface sealing and soil structure. When application exceeds infiltration or storage capacity, water can collect or flow downhill.

Check for physical problems first

Inspect for broken heads, leaks, spray aimed at pavement, misaligned nozzles and low areas. Water accumulating beside a single head may indicate a local fault rather than a scheduling problem. Check the complete zone before changing runtimes.

Consider slope and soil condition

Sloping ground gives water less time to infiltrate before it moves downslope. Compacted soil, crusting and saturated conditions can also contribute. Surface puddling does not reliably identify the underlying soil texture or quantify infiltration capacity.

Cycle-and-soak scheduling

Instead of one long watering period, cycle-and-soak divides irrigation into shorter applications with pauses between them. This can reduce runoff when soil needs time to absorb applied water. Suitable cycle lengths and rest periods depend on the site, precipitation rate and soil conditions; there is no universal schedule.

Match nozzle precipitation rates

Different sprinkler types and nozzles can apply water at different rates. Combining mismatched heads on a single zone can leave some areas wet and others dry. Manufacturer precipitation-rate data and a properly conducted catch-can assessment can inform improvements.

Water responsibly

  • Observe runoff during an irrigation cycle and note when it begins.
  • Inspect heads and fittings before changing controller settings.
  • Review precipitation rate and distribution uniformity.
  • Adjust scheduling cautiously and check soil moisture in the root zone.
  • Follow local water restrictions and prevent overspray onto sidewalks or roads.
