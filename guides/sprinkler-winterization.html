How to winterize a sprinkler system without damaging equipment

Independent maintenance guide · November 2026. General guidance for freezing climates in the United States; specific procedures vary by system and local code.

Safety first: Compressed air can injure people and damage pipes, valves and sprinkler heads. Never guess at blowout pressure or airflow settings. If you do not have the system manufacturer's procedure and suitable equipment, hire a qualified irrigation professional.

Know which parts are exposed to freezing

Identify the controller, water supply shutoff, backflow prevention assembly, aboveground piping, valves, drip lines and low points. Some systems drain by gravity; others require additional procedures. An underground pipe is not automatically protected from freezing.

Check the backflow preventer instructions

Backflow assemblies protect drinking water and may be subject to local inspection and testing requirements. Follow the exact manufacturer's winterization instructions and applicable plumbing codes. Do not dismantle, bypass or modify the device without appropriate qualifications.

Protect the controller and sensors

Many smart irrigation controllers offer an off, standby or rain-delay setting. Verify whether schedules remain stored and whether the controller needs continuous power for memory or freeze-related features. A software setting does not remove water from pipes. See our controller checklist for compatibility and offline-operation questions.

Drainage and blowout are not interchangeable

Automatic-drain, manual-drain and compressed-air systems have different requirements. Inappropriate air pressure or flow can damage components. Consult manufacturer instructions for each installed component; do not assume a generic air-compressor setting is safe.

What to inspect before winter

  • Document the location of shutoff valves and the system layout.
  • Look for leaking fittings, broken heads and damaged valve boxes.
  • Check exposed hose bibs, filters and aboveground components.
  • Review manufacturer instructions for rain and soil-moisture sensors.
  • Record any repairs needed before spring startup.

Spring startup matters too

When freezing risk has passed, restore water gradually according to the manufacturer's instructions, inspect for leaks and confirm zone operation. Check backflow devices as required by local rules. A professional inspection may be worthwhile after a severe winter or if water pressure behaves unexpectedly.

Before hiring a winterization service

Ask whether the provider has experience with your controller, valve and backflow equipment; what is included in the service; and whether they follow manufacturer limits and local requirements.
