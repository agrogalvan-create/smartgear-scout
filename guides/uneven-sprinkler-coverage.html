Uneven sprinkler coverage: what to check before replacing heads

Independent technical guide · November 2026. Educational troubleshooting, not a field-tested product review or a site-specific irrigation design.

Look at the pattern, not just the dry spots

Dry patches may reflect poor distribution, compacted soil, slope, runoff, plant competition or inconsistent irrigation scheduling. Observe a complete irrigation cycle when safe and permitted. Note whether dry areas appear between heads, near boundaries or at the far end of a zone.

Check nozzle and head compatibility

Different nozzle models can have different precipitation rates, radii and operating pressure requirements. Mixing incompatible spray and rotor heads on one zone may create uneven application. Compare installed nozzles with manufacturer charts, rather than assuming matching appearance means matching output.

Evaluate pressure under operation

Low operating pressure may reduce throw distance and coverage. Excessive pressure can create misting and increase wind drift. Measure at appropriate accessible points with suitable instruments and compare results with manufacturer operating ranges. See our pressure and flow planning guide.

Inspect physical problems

  • Blocked or tilted heads, grass growth or obstacles
  • Clogged nozzles and damaged filters
  • Visible leaks, broken risers or fittings
  • Incorrect arc adjustments and overspray onto hard surfaces
  • Wind exposure during irrigation

Understand head spacing and precipitation rate

Sprinkler patterns often rely on overlapping coverage, but actual spacing must match nozzle specifications and site conditions. A head's maximum advertised throw is not proof of uniform coverage at that spacing. Precipitation rate must also be compatible with soil infiltration and slope to avoid runoff.

Simple distribution observations

For a professional evaluation, a catch-can test can help compare applied water across a zone. Use consistent containers, a defined run period and appropriate measurement procedures; account for wind and evaporation. Uneven collected depths suggest a distribution problem, but do not by themselves identify its cause.

When a controller change helps—and when it does not

A smart controller can adjust runtimes using weather and scheduling inputs, but it cannot correct a blocked nozzle, incompatible head layout or inadequate hydraulic capacity. Start with physical system performance. See our smart controller checklist and sprinkler versus drip comparison.
