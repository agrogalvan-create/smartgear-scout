Understand the three different functions

EquipmentWhat it can doImportant limitation
Point leak sensorDetects moisture at a specific location and may sound an alarm or send a notificationCannot detect a leak outside its sensing area
Flow-monitoring deviceMay identify unusual water use patterns in the supply lineSmall or intermittent leaks can be difficult to distinguish from normal use
Automatic shutoff valveCan stop incoming water when its trigger criteria are metRequires compatible plumbing, power and maintenance; may not stop all sources of water damage

Five checks before buying

  1. Choose coverage areas. Consider washing machines, dishwashers, water heaters, sinks and any low points where water accumulates.
  2. Decide whether an alert is enough. A sensor can notify you, but remote alerts are not the same as stopping the water supply.
  3. Confirm connectivity. Check whether the device requires Wi-Fi, a hub, a cloud account or a paid service; understand behavior during outages.
  4. Check installation and power. Confirm battery replacement, electrical safety, pipe dimensions and any required professional installation.
  5. Test regularly. Follow the manufacturer's test procedure, check notification settings and ensure household members know the manual shutoff location.

Comparing candidates

Our research queue includes Moen Flo, Resideo L1 and Govee water sensors. These products may serve different purposes; we are not treating them as equivalent or ranking them until their current official specifications are verified.

What this guide does not promise

No leak detection system guarantees prevention of flooding, pipe failures or water damage. A shutoff device installed on one supply line may not control other water sources, including roof leaks, drains or groundwater.
