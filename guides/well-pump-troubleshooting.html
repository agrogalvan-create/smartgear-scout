Well pump problems: what to check before replacing the pump

Independent educational guide · November 2026. This is not an on-site diagnosis or electrical repair procedure. No equipment was physically tested for this article.

Safety: Well pump systems combine electricity, pressurized water and potentially contaminated water. Turn to a qualified well contractor or electrician for electrical testing, pressure-switch adjustments, tank service and pump removal. Do not open energized equipment or bypass safety devices.

Low pressure or poor flow

Low pressure can result from a restricted filter, leaking pipe, pressure-control issue, low well yield or pump performance problem. Compare conditions at several fixtures and note whether symptoms appear only during simultaneous use. A replacement pump selected without identifying the cause may not solve the problem.

Rapid on-and-off cycling

Frequent short cycles may be associated with pressure tank issues, leaks, control settings or an undersized system. Repeated cycling can accelerate wear. Record how frequently the pump starts and whether pressure falls while fixtures are closed; have a professional inspect the tank and controls.

Air, sputtering or cloudy water

Air at taps may indicate a plumbing leak, changing well water level or other system issue. Cloudiness or changes in odor, color or taste warrant appropriate water-quality evaluation. Do not assume the pump alone is responsible or that water is safe without testing.

Dry running and declining well yield

A pump cannot sustainably deliver more water than the well and aquifer can provide. Long pumping periods may increase drawdown. A qualified contractor can evaluate static and pumping levels, recovery, well condition and dry-run protection.

What to record for a service call

  • When the problem began and whether it is constant or intermittent
  • Observed pressure readings, if safely available at an existing gauge
  • Pump start frequency and any alarm indications
  • Recent drought, power events, filter changes or plumbing work
  • Changes in water appearance and any laboratory water-test results

When replacement may be appropriate

If a professional confirms pump failure or inadequate capacity, compare models using required flow and total dynamic head, not horsepower alone. See our well pump buying guide for the selection method and our home pressure troubleshooting checklist for fixture and piping restrictions.
