How to choose a well pump using flow and total dynamic head

Independent technical buying guide · November 2026. General educational information, not a site-specific design or hands-on pump test.

Important: Verify well construction, sustainable yield and local water regulations before buying. Well-pump installation involves electrical, sanitary and water-supply risks; use appropriately qualified professionals.

1. Determine realistic water demand

Estimate simultaneous household flow in gallons per minute (GPM), allowing for irrigation or other uses that may operate together. Do not size a pump simply by summing every fixture's maximum rating or selecting the highest horsepower available.

2. Distinguish static water level from pumping water level

Static water level is measured when the well is at rest. Pumping water level is the depth to water while the well is producing at a given rate. Drawdown is the difference between those levels. Pump selection should consider the pumping level at the design flow, not just the static depth or total drilled depth.

3. Estimate total dynamic head

Total dynamic head (TDH) represents the energy the pump must add at the intended flow. For a typical pressurized system, account for vertical lift from the pumping water level to the delivery point, required delivery pressure and pipe/friction losses. In water, 1 psi is approximately 2.31 feet of head. Confirm the appropriate datum and system arrangement before adding terms.

4. A simplified example

Suppose the pumping water level is 100 feet below the delivery elevation, the target delivery pressure is 40 psi, and estimated pipe and fitting losses at the design flow equal 15 feet of head. The planning estimate is 100 + (40 × 2.31) + 15 = approximately 207 feet TDH. The actual pump must deliver the required GPM at roughly that head; this example does not select a model or account for all installation details.

5. Compare the operating point with a pump curve

Find the intersection of design flow and TDH on a manufacturer's published performance curve. A maximum-flow figure typically describes a different operating condition than maximum-head capability. Verify pump efficiency range, motor rating, voltage, starting current and the complete operating envelope.

6. Confirm the well can sustain the withdrawal

Review pumping-test results and seasonal water-level behavior. If the well's sustainable yield is lower than demand, a storage tank and appropriately controlled refill strategy may be needed. A larger pump does not increase the aquifer's sustainable yield and may worsen drawdown or cause dry running.

7. Include system controls and protections

  • Confirm pressure-tank sizing and pressure-switch or variable-speed control requirements.
  • Check dry-run, overload and short-circuit protection.
  • Review check-valve arrangements and sanitary sealing requirements.
  • Confirm cable sizing, voltage drop, grounding and installation codes.
  • Consider water quality, sediment and compatibility with well materials.

Buying checklist

Request the pump curve, design GPM, estimated TDH, pumping water level, well yield, electrical specifications, control requirements, warranty and service availability. If pressure boosting is needed after a storage tank, also read how to size a booster pump; that is a different application from lifting groundwater from a well.
